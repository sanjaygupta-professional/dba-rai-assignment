The shared responsible pattern

AI prepares. Human decides. Human owns the adverse call.

In every domain, the AI's most adverse action is to route for review or pend for info. It never denies, never fails a student, never declines a loan. The human always owns that call.

⚙
AI prepares
Assembles evidence, maps criteria, drafts a recommendation with citations
→
⚖
Human decides
Reviews, verifies citations, confirms or overrides — the only step we compress
→
⚑
Human owns the adverse call
Every denial, failing grade, or decline is made by a licensed human with independent judgment
Healthcare: clinician owns the denial
Education: instructor owns the grade
Finance: underwriter owns the decline
How it was built

A build-then-stress-test loop.

A quality ratchet between every milestone — any dimension scoring below 3 blocks progress until remediated.

1
Absorb context
Read the assignment brief, course outline, and DARWIN lecture. Key signal: prototype ≠ product — validates the business case, not the technology.
2
Design the evaluator panel
Built a scoring instrument: Evaluator A "Sunkad" (Prototype Purist) + Evaluator B "Adversarial CFO" (SWAT) + Gate-0 checklist. Ratchet: any dimension < 3 blocks progress.
3
Choose 3 domains
Healthcare, Education, Finance — all sharing one responsible pattern, each with a distinct prototype shape and RAI surface.
4
Author in parallel
Three independent subagents, each blind to the others and pre-loaded with the panel rubric, wrote a full ideation package.
5
Gate-1 adversarial scoring
Three harsh, independent evaluators scored each package. All three passed — but the panel found circular ROI math, bundled hypotheses, and underpowered kill thresholds.
6
Harden
Two parallel revisers fixed Healthcare + Education — unbundling hypotheses, demoting underpowered kills to directional flags, replacing circular numbers with external anchors — without inflating the prototype into a product.
7
Re-score
Re-ran the adversarial panel. Healthcare 46→48, Education robust at 46 with worst weaknesses resolved.
8
Pending
Optionally harden Finance; run the Gate-3 independent model (Gemini + Codex, blind); down-select and build the presentation.
The evaluator panel

Built first, so the work wasn't graded by its own writer.

Two standing evaluators mirror exactly what loses points in the rubric. At least one is always independent of the author.

Evaluator A — "Sunkad"
Prototype Purist
Kill question: "Did you build a product and call it a prototype?"
A1Prototype-not-product
A2Right tool (no LLM-overuse)
A3Focused 1–2 hypotheses
A4Fail-fast design
A5Validates business case, not tech
Evaluator B — "Adversarial CFO"
SWAT / So-What
Kill question: "If it works perfectly, does the business make money — and is the number proven?"
B1Sharp value tied to revenue/cost
B2Defensible Fermi ROI
B3Distinct business metric
B4Precise, reproducible technical metric
B5A specific kill threshold
Gate 0 — a 10-point completeness checklist runs first.   The ratchet — every dimension scored 1–5; any dimension below 3 blocks progress until remediated and re-scored.   Independent model (Gemini + Codex, blind) reserved for the Gate-3 final.
